# Banked predictor, response predecode, and FixBranch

Date: 2026-07-24

## Objective

Remove the structural branch-recovery bubble without putting an asynchronous
predictor or instruction decode on the L1I response timing path. Preserve the
fixed fetch4/decode3/commit3 architecture, exact redirects, uncached AXI
ordering, and automatically generated Verilog-only implementation policy.

## Design

- `OooBankedFetchPredictor` uses four synchronous banks. Each bank owns a
  128-entry BTB and a 1024-entry two-bit PHT; all banks share an eight-bit GHR
  and eight-entry speculative/architectural RAS.
- The predictor lookup starts with the accepted translation request. Commit
  trains direction, target, branch type, and RAS state. Conditional branches
  retain BTFNT until their direction entry is known to be trained.
- `OooFetchPredecode` is generated beside the registered L1I or uncached AXI
  response. FixBranch compares the carried metadata with the early prediction
  without decoding the instruction opcode on the response-state path.
- A wrong cached handoff is blocked before L1I acceptance. An uncached handoff
  that has already entered AXI is tagged and drained before the corrected
  request can consume its response.
- `refillReplayPending` returns an already-complete same-line group on the next
  cycle. This breaks the response-to-response predecode path that previously
  prevented 100 MHz closure.
- ROB branch payload and predictor metadata are carried to the oldest committed
  branch update. Response cold learning adds at most one stable direct branch.

## Rejected sub-experiments

- Expanding the instruction buffer from 8 to 16 entries produced no cycle
  reduction and increased pointer/storage cost.
- Exact overlap reservation changed only 31 local cycles and was reverted.
- Killing a stale same-line L1I refill reduced observed drop cycles but
  regressed `func_lab19` to 553,608 cycles by destroying useful prefetch.
- Combinational 32/128-entry target or direction tables reached 538,501/536,483
  cycles but failed timing at -2.658/-3.275 ns and were rejected.

## Compliance and limits

All CPU behavior changes are Scala/SpinalHDL source. `rtl/mycpu_top.v` was
regenerated by the locked package gate and byte-compared by publish-check; no
handwritten production Verilog, timing exception, simulator shortcut, or
workload-specific behavior was added. Local `func_lab19` is not evidence for
remote `func58` or `perf20`; those jobs remain required before promotion to a
performance baseline.
