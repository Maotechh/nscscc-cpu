# Divider wakeup timing cut

Date: 2026-07-26

## Objective

Remove the complete-SoC critical path from the variable-latency divider result tag through
same-cycle issue-queue wakeup and oldest-ready selection. Preserve precise completion, physical
register writeback, and the one-cycle ALU/fixed-latency MUL wakeup paths.

## Routed diagnosis

Commit `fbe01256b376ecfd3367002b16e82d0f35900005` completed the locked Vivado 2023.2
`func58` implementation at 100 MHz with no DRC errors, but timing did not close: WNS was
`-0.780680 ns` and TNS was `-393.443848 ns`. The worst path started at
`execution/divider/uop_pdst_reg[1]`, crossed divider completion arbitration and issue-queue
selection, and ended at an issue-queue immediate register. Its 10.319 ns data delay had 12 logic
levels and 8.272 ns of routing delay (80.2%).

The same package passed the remote `func58` board check, but that result is independent of timing
closure. The negative WNS remains a correctness risk at 100 MHz.

## ysyx comparison

The reference is ysyx-workbench branch `la32r-linux`, commit
`8baa79a24989e2a5b7ebee76b1abcd2be1b10c7a`. Its issue queue wakes one-cycle operations when they
issue and fixed-latency multiply operations at their known stage. Variable-latency divide does not
broadcast its raw completion tag into issue selection; it wakes dependants through registered
writeback. This classification gives the timing cut without adding a second backend protocol.

## Design

- Direct wakeup now contains only accepted one-cycle operations and fixed-latency MUL.
- Divider completion uses the ROB registered completion/wakeup boundary.
- On the execution port shared by DIV and ALU, an older divider completion suppresses a younger
  direct ALU wake for that cycle because the completion owns the lane.
- If a registered ROB wake and a younger direct wake reach the same lane together, the registered
  wake has priority. The younger completion remains in the ROB path and wakes on the following
  cycle, so neither physical tag is lost.
- Flush stays out of the narrow direct-wakeup event. Issue queues already give flush priority over
  ready-bit updates.

An initial cut gave direct wake priority over the registered wake. Directed testing and official
simulation exposed a deadlock: a divider dependant could permanently miss its wake when the next
ALU operation completed on the same port. Registered-wake priority is the functional fix, and a
new collision test locks the behavior.

All CPU implementation changes are in Scala/SpinalHDL. `rtl/mycpu_top.v` was regenerated by the
repository publisher; no Verilog was edited by hand.

## Promotion boundary

Standalone synthesis does not include board placement, routing congestion, or the complete SoC.
This candidate must be committed so the locked evaluator can build an exact 40-character revision.
It is not a new timing baseline until the 100 MHz full design has non-negative routed WNS. Only
after timing closure and a passing `func58` job may the same committed candidate proceed to three
real `perf20` runs.

## Rule audit

The change remains inside the CPU, preserves the official 49-port interface and LA32R behavior,
and adds no accelerator, answer table, simulation dependency, or handwritten Verilog. The generated
RTL hash is locked by both replacement and lint manifests.
